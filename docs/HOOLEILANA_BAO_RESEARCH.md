# Hoʻoleilana: An Individual Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO) Relic in the Local Universe

## Abstract & Executive Summary

In September 2023, R. Brent Tully, Cullan Howlett, and Daniel Pomarède published the identification of **Hoʻoleilana** (*The Astrophysical Journal*, 954:169), proposing it as an unusually prominent individual realization of a **Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO)** shell in the local universe. Centered at $z \approx 0.068$ ($cz \approx 20,400\text{ km/s}$, comoving distance $d \approx 250\text{ Mpc}/h \approx 820\text{ Mly}$), Hoʻoleilana forms a roughly spherical, shell-like concentration of galaxies with a radius of $R \approx 155\text{ Mpc}$ ($\approx 1\text{ billion light-years}$ in shell diameter).

This discovery fundamentally recontextualizes several major structures in classical cosmic cartography:
1. **Boötes Supercluster** resides near the geometric center of the shell.
2. **Sloan Great Wall**, **CfA2 Great Wall** (Coma-Hercules segment), and the **Hercules Complex** form cohesive arcs along the spherical shell boundary.
3. **Laniākea Supercluster** ($\approx 500\text{ Mly}$ across) reaches outward toward the near edge of Hoʻoleilana.

## 1. Physical Foundations: Baryon Acoustic Oscillations

### 1.1 Primordial Acoustic Propagation ($t < 380,000\text{ yr}$)
Prior to cosmological recombination ($z > 1090$), baryonic matter and photons were tightly coupled by Thomson scattering into a single relativistic fluid. Primordial scalar density perturbations $\delta(\mathbf{x})$ generated by quantum fluctuations during cosmic inflation set up localized gravitational potential wells $\Phi(\mathbf{x})$.

As gravity compressed the plasma inward, radiation pressure resisted collapse and drove an outward-propagating spherical sound wave at the relativistic sound speed $c_{s}(z)$:

$$
c_{s}(z) = \frac{c}{\sqrt{3 \left(1 + \frac{3 \Omega_{b}}{4 \Omega_{\gamma} (1+z)}\right)}}
$$

where $\Omega_{b}$ is the baryon density parameter, $\Omega_{\gamma}$ is the photon radiation density parameter, and $c$ is the speed of light in vacuum.

### 1.2 Drag Epoch & Freezing ($z_{d} \approx 1060$)
At the drag epoch ($z_{d} \approx 1060$, approximately $380,000\text{ years}$ after the Big Bang), photons decoupled from baryons, and the photon mean free path expanded to cosmological scales. The radiation pressure plummeted to zero, freezing the outward propagation of the acoustic pressure wave at the **comoving sound horizon** $r_{s}(z_{d})$:

$$
r_{s}(z_{d}) = \int_{z_{d}}^{\infty} \frac{c_{s}(z)}{H(z)} \mathrm{d}z
$$

In standard flat $\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}$ cosmology, $H(z)$ is the Hubble expansion parameter:

$$
H(z) = H_{0} \sqrt{\Omega_{m,0} (1+z)^3 + \Omega_{r,0} (1+z)^4 + \Omega_{\Lambda,0}}
$$

Evaluating the integral with Planck 2018 cosmological parameters ($\Omega_{b,0} h^2 \approx 0.02237$, $\Omega_{c,0} h^2 \approx 0.1200$, $h \approx 0.6736$) yields the fiducial acoustic sound horizon:

$$
r_{d} = r_{s}(z_{d}) \approx 147.09 \pm 0.26\text{ Mpc} \approx 98.9\text{ Mpc}/h
$$

### 1.3 Gravitational Evolution & Galaxy Condensation
Although the dark matter perturbation remained concentrated at the central origin, gravitational attraction between the central dark matter peak and the spherical baryonic shell equalized the perturbations over cosmic time. Galaxies preferentially formed both at the central node and along the spherical acoustic shell boundary, producing a characteristic spatial correlation peak at separation $s \approx r_{d}$.

## 2. Statistical BAO vs. Individual Realization

### 2.1 Classical Statistical Detection
Conventionally, the BAO feature is observed statistically by evaluating the two-point spatial galaxy correlation function $\xi(s)$ or matter power spectrum $P(k)$ over millions of galaxy pairs across hundreds of cubic gigaparsecs (e.g., SDSS, BOSS, eBOSS, DESI):

$$
\xi(s) = \int \frac{k^2 \mathrm{d}k}{2\pi^2} P(k) j_{0}(ks)
$$

The acoustic peak appears as a subtle $\sim 1\%$ enhancement in galaxy pair clustering at $s \approx 100\,h^{-1}\text{Mpc}$.

### 2.2 Hoʻoleilana as an Individual Realization
Tully, Howlett, & Pomarède (2023) demonstrated that Hoʻoleilana represents an unusually strong individual realization of this spherical shell geometry. Using 3D reconstructions from CosmicFlows-4 and redshift surveys, they performed statistical mock tests comparing models with and without the acoustic peak:
- Observed shell radius: $R \approx 155\text{ Mpc}$ ($105\,h^{-1}\text{Mpc}$ for $h = 0.70$).
- Central concentration: Boötes Supercluster core.
- Shell constituents: Sloan Great Wall, CfA2 Great Wall, Hercules Complex.
- Mock significance: The probability of finding such an overdense spherical shell in Gaussian random fields devoid of acoustic oscillations is $p < 0.001$.

Hoʻoleilana introduces a fundamental paradigm shift in cosmic taxonomy:

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Physical Origin** | Non-linear gravitational collapse & velocity streamlines | Relativistic plasma sound wave frozen at recombination |
| **Epoch of Genesis** | Late universe ($z < 1$, ongoing dynamical growth) | Infant universe ($z \approx 1060$, $t \approx 380,000\text{ yr}$) |
| **Defining Metric** | Zero-velocity divergence watershed boundary ($\nabla \cdot \mathbf{v} = 0$) | Fixed physical radius shell ($R \approx 155\text{ Mpc}$) |

| **Typical Scale** | $R \sim 80\text{ Mpc}$ (Laniākea), $R \sim 150\text{ Mpc}$ (Shapley) | $R \approx 155\text{ Mpc}$ ($1\text{ billion light-years}$ diameter) |

## 4. Cosmological Implications & Hubble Tension

If Hoʻoleilana is interpreted as a standard cosmological ruler with shell radius matching the acoustic sound horizon ($R = r_{d}$), its angular separation $\theta_{\text{BAO}}$ and measured redshift $z = 0.068$ constrain the comoving angular diameter distance:

$$
D_{M}(z) = \frac{c}{H_{0}} \int_{0}^{z} \frac{\mathrm{d}z'}{E(z')}
$$

Using the fiducial sound horizon calibrated from early-universe physics, Tully et al. inferred:

$$
H_{0} = 76.9^{+8.2}_{-4.8}\text{ km/s/Mpc}
$$

### Scientific Caution & Epistemic Status
- **Central Value**: Sits on the higher local-expansion side of the Hubble tension divide (comparable to SH0ES Cepheid/SN Ia measurements $H_{0} = 73.04 \pm 1.04\text{ km/s/Mpc}$ rather than Planck early-universe $\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}$ $67.4 \pm 0.5\text{ km/s/Mpc}$).
- **Uncertainty Range**: The asymmetric error bounds ($+8.2 / -4.8\text{ km/s/Mpc}$) span both sides of the divide, meaning Hoʻoleilana cannot single-handedly resolve the tension.
- **Classification**: Epistemic code `◌ Candidate Relic / Low-Precision BAO`.
