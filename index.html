❓ What is "Epistemic Code" & Scientific Uncertainty?

Epistemic (from Greek epistēmē, "knowledge"): In physics and data science, Epistemic Uncertainty represents limitations in our observational knowledge, distinct from natural physical randomness.

🔭 Why Does Epistemic Code Exist in CosmicFlows-4?

When mapping the universe across 400 Mpc/h, astronomers cannot measure every galaxy with infinite precision:
  • Tully-Fisher & SNe Distance Scatter: Galaxy rotation velocities give metric distances with ~15% statistical error.
  • Zone of Avoidance (ZoA): The Milky Way's dense dust plane hides ~20% of the extragalactic sky.
  • Wiener Filter Posterior Variance (σδ): In dense data regions, the reconstruction is ~95% certain; in sparse deep space, it smoothly reverts to the mean cosmic density.

● Observed: Real astronomical telescope measurements (56k galaxy redshift/TF data).

◌ Posterior Sample: Constrained realization testing error variance bounds.

In short: Epistemic code tells scientists whether a filament or void is a definitively measured physical structure or a mathematical interpolation!

📘 Cosmography & Velocity Field Interactive Primer

1. Peculiar Velocity vs. Uniform Hubble Expansion

The total observed recession velocity of a galaxy (cz) is composed of the uniform, isotropic expansion of spacetime (H0d) plus its physical peculiar velocity (vpec):

cz = H0d + vpec · r̂  ⇔  vpec = cz − H0d

Peculiar velocity is driven entirely by gravitational attractions from mass overdensities (clusters and filaments) and repulsive divergence from underdense cosmic voids.

2. The Laniakea Supercluster & Cosmic Watersheds

Defined by Tully et al. (2014) in Nature, Laniakea (“Immeasurable Heaven”) is our home gravitational basin of attraction spanning ~160 Mpc (~520 million light-years) and containing over 100,000 galaxies.

• Watershed Boundary: The zero-velocity divergence surface separating streamlines flowing toward the Great Attractor (Norma Cluster ACO 3627) from those falling toward the Perseus-Pisces or Shapley basins.

3. The Dipole Repeller & Cosmic Void Dynamics

Discovered by Hoffman, Courtois, Tully, & Pomarède (2017) in Nature Astronomy, the Dipole Repeller is a massive underdense void acting as a cosmological divergence center.

• Repulsive Outflow: Because this region contains far less mass than the cosmic mean density (δ < 0), the surrounding dense superclusters pull galaxies away from it, generating an effective repulsive velocity outflow of ~250 km/s aligned directly opposite the Shapley Attractor.

🪐 Hoʻoleilana: Primordial Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO) Primer

1. What is a Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO)?

A Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO) is a fossil sound wave from the infant universe. Before atoms formed (t < 380,000 years), normal matter (baryons) and photons existed as an ultra-hot, coupled plasma. Gravity pulled matter inward toward dense spots, while radiation pressure pushed violently outward, generating spherical acoustic pressure waves that traveled outward at relativistic speeds:

cs(z) = c / √( 3 [ 1 + (3 Ωb / 4 Ωγ) (1+z)−1 ] )

When the universe cooled enough for photons to decouple from matter (the drag epoch at z ≈ 1060), this outward sound wave froze in place at the comoving sound horizon (rd ≈ 147–155 Mpc). Over billions of years, galaxies formed preferentially along both the central dense core and this spherical acoustic rim.

2. What Hoʻoleilana Actually Is

In September 2023, R. Brent Tully, Cullan Howlett, and Daniel Pomarède published the discovery of Hoʻoleilana (The Astrophysical Journal 954:169). They identified a roughly spherical shell of galaxies with a radius of ≈ 155 Mpc (≈ 505 million light-years), giving it a diameter of nearly 1 billion light-years. Its center lies ≈ 820 million light-years from Earth.

• Boötes Supercluster: Sits near the geometric core of the shell.
• Constituent Walls: Structures astronomers named separately—including the Sloan Great Wall, CfA2 Great Wall, and the Hercules Complex—appear to be pieces of this single, gargantuan spherical shell.
• Laniākea Scale Comparison: Laniākea is 500 million light-years across, yet reaches only toward the near perimeter of Hoʻoleilana.

3. Cosmic Address Paradigm: Dynamic Basins vs. Acoustic Relics

This discovery shifts cosmic cartography from a static classification hierarchy to an origin-based taxonomy:

Dynamic Flow Basin (Laniākea):
Defined dynamically by galaxy peculiar velocities collapsing toward gravitational attractors today.
→ Tells us where matter is flowing now.
Primordial Acoustic Relic (Hoʻoleilana):
Surviving spatial imprint of a relativistic plasma pressure wave frozen at recombination.
→ Tells us where a sound wave traveled > 13 billion years ago.

4. The Hubble Tension & Scientific Caution

Interpreting Hoʻoleilana as a standard acoustic ruler yields an inferred expansion rate:

H0 ≈ 76.9 +8.2−4.8 km/s/Mpc

This central value aligns with local late-universe measurements (SH0ES Cepheids / SN Ia at 73.04 ± 1.04 km/s/Mpc) rather than Planck early-universe ΛCDM (67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc). However, the uncertainty (+8.2 / −4.8) is too broad to single-handedly resolve the Hubble tension.

Scientific Caution: The statistical BAO phenomenon is confirmed beyond doubt across millions of galaxies. However, isolating an individual nearby cosmic shell carries statistical variance. Hoʻoleilana is a compelling scientific proposal, marked with epistemic status ◌ Candidate Relic / Low-Precision BAO.
